What each ticket fixes

Shared parser: day-first tenant dates are read month first. For example, R-41200 reports 7 April becoming 4 July. Correct the shared parsing contract and test day-first locales across app, email, and booking-v2 form paths. A booking-v3 tenant’s app or email report still belongs here; form version alone does not determine the cause.

Booking-v3 hint: its date field prints MM/DD/YYYY to day-first tenants while the form reads input in the tenant’s date order. The hint and interpretation disagree. Make the hint match the form’s locale-aware interpretation, and test the form route separately. A shared-parser fix alone does not establish that this mismatch is fixed.

Five desk-noted reports describe one-day moves or related timing anomalies: R-41202, R-41209, R-41236, R-41344, and R-41620. The supplied classifier places them inside the parser total. Keep them there without double counting, but investigate them separately within that ticket. Their wording does not establish a timezone root cause or justify claiming a third confirmed cause. Capture original input, expected and actual dates, timezone, locale, and path before deciding whether to split again.

When reports arrived and when the desk merged them

Received and merged are different events. Reports arrived from April; the large early-July merge combines an existing backlog. It does not show that the failures began in July, that July had an equivalent arrival spike, or that merging fixed anything. New reports continue through the export date. Inspect both series when deciding priority.

Stop the same merge from happening again

The triage page says to merge on normalized subject alone and to merge when unsure. Decision 0001, accepted in June 2024, records that rule. Its age and the rendered counterexamples require a fresh decision. The accepted decision remains unchanged as history.

Proposed replacement: use subject matches only to find candidates. Compare tenant locale, form version, channel, symptoms, and reproduction evidence. Merge only with evidence of the same cause. Keep unknown tenants and unresolved timing anomalies in review; an unknown cause is not evidence of duplication. Route new reports while the old backlog is being reconciled so they are not silently merged back into the umbrella ticket.
